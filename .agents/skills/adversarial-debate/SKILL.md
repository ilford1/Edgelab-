# Adversarial Debate

Use a routed subgraph centered on the target claim. Ask the router for supporting findings, contradictions, mechanisms, alternatives, confounders, and tests; do not load unrelated market research.

## Debate frame

1. Restate the claim at the correct level: observation, hypothesis, mechanism, edge, or strategy.
2. Give the strongest case **for** it, including the participant-level mechanism and why information might not already be in price.
3. Give the strongest case **against** it. Attack the actual claim, not a weaker version.
4. Offer at least one simpler alternative explanation and one data/measurement failure when plausible.
5. List the assumptions on which the disagreement turns.
6. Name the smallest observation or experiment that separates the leading explanations.
7. Conclude with a provisional position and what would change it.

Maintain separate belief lanes for phenomenon existence, prediction, incremental information, and exploitability. Use qualitative levels unless numbers genuinely clarify uncertainty; never present invented precision.

When the user defeats an objection with evidence, concede it explicitly and update. When the thesis is well supported, say so and focus only on material remaining risks. Truth, not disagreement, is the objective.

Do not call something an edge merely because its mechanism sounds plausible. Do not use debate as a substitute for data.
