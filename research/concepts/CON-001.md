# CON-001 — Impulse exhaustion

## Definition

A state in which maintaining directional price progress requires increasing flow, time, or liquidity consumption. Exhaustion is not equivalent to reversal and must not be labeled solely from a future-known peak or trough.

## Measurement boundary

Research may use retrospective impulse labels for analysis, but any deployable state estimate must specify what was observable at time `t`. Candidate evidence includes declining response efficiency, failed continuation, replenishment, and changing spread/depth, each tested separately.

## Open risks

Scale choice, volatility normalization, event segmentation, and exchange mechanics can create apparent exhaustion without predictive information.

The speed-and-density framing in `KI-20260913-004` narrows the real-time condition: elevated activity or trade-arrival intensity is not exhaustion on its own. A candidate exhaustion sequence requires continued effort while executable price velocity or response efficiency falls. That state may lead to pause, reversal, or renewed continuation, so the future outcome must be tested rather than embedded in the label.

`KI-20260913-005` adds a possible book-side transition: the consumed side first retreats under taking, then persistently replenishes or reconcentrates while taking continues and price response weakens. The transition after maximum stress is the candidate exhaustion state; the maximum spread or depth dispersion alone is not a reversal label.
