# OQ-002 — Does joint speed and density identify scalpable impulses?

## Why it matters

The framework captured in `KI-20260913-004` proposes that accelerating market activity plus fast price movement identifies moments worth scalping, while high volume per unit movement helps distinguish thick from thin moves. If true after costs, this could improve event selection for short-horizon research. At present it is an untested idea, not an edge or trade signal.

## Current evidence

Only the user's conceptual observation is available. No venue, instrument, horizon, smoothing rule, event sample, baseline, effect estimate, or execution record was supplied.

The components must be measured separately:

- Signed price velocity: `log(P_t / P_{t-h}) / h` using a declared executable price and causal horizon.
- Trade intensity: trade count per unit time; volume intensity: quantity or notional per unit time.
- Density: total volume divided by absolute price movement plus a declared floor.
- Response efficiency: signed price progress per same-side aggressive flow, as in `SIG-001`.

High density is ambiguous between absorption, two-sided churn, market making, and measurement artifacts. Low density with high price velocity may instead reflect a thin book. A two-period smoother is not fully specified until its input, filter type, and update clock are declared.

## Resolution test

On causally reconstructed trade-and-quote data, freeze the formulas and compare states formed by independently normalized price-velocity acceleration and trade-intensity acceleration. Condition on spread, depth, volatility, trend, venue, time of day, and listing age. Measure near-horizon continuation, reversal, realized volatility, maximum favorable/adverse excursion, fill probability, and net outcome after fees, slippage, latency, and impact.

Compare at least four cells: fast price/high activity, fast price/ordinary activity, slow price/high activity, and matched ordinary controls. Then test whether rising density means absorption by requiring continued same-side aggressive flow with declining executable price response and, where available, replenishing depth.

The claim is weakened if trade intensity adds no incremental information beyond price velocity and volatility, if the apparent effect is caused by fixed-window overlap or smoothing, or if gross movement cannot be captured at executable prices.
