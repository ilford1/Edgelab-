# OQ-003 — Does consumed-side depth recovery predict LTF reversal?

## Why it matters

The observation in `KI-20260913-005` proposes that heavy taker flow first opens the spread and pushes liquidity away on the consumed side, then creates a fade opportunity when that side begins countering the flow. If the transition is observable before price reverses and survives execution costs, it could refine the descriptive exhaustion state in `CON-001`. It is not currently a validated edge or trade signal.

## Current evidence

Only the user's commentary is available. The venue, instrument, LTF horizon, event count, sampling clock, book depth, normalization, smoothing, and outcomes are unknown.

“Standard deviation of price depth” has at least three non-equivalent interpretations:

1. Quantity-weighted dispersion of resting price levels from the same-side best quote or mid within a fixed bps band.
2. Dispersion of displayed quantities across book levels.
3. Time-series variability of aggregate side depth across snapshots.

The commentary says consumed-side dispersion increases as liquidity retreats, but also associates countering activity with decreasing dispersion. A coherent candidate sequence is therefore **expansion followed by rollover**, not a static peak rule. For aggressive buying: ask-side retreat and spread expansion first; then persistent near-touch ask replenishment or falling ask dispersion while buy taking remains elevated and price response decays. Mirror for aggressive selling.

## Resolution test

Reconstruct synchronized trade and order-book events with sequence-gap checks. Prespecify the consumed side from signed taker flow and calculate each dispersion interpretation separately within fixed level-count and fixed-bps bands. Normalize for price, tick size, typical depth, spread, volatility, trade intensity, and impulse age.

Trigger only on information available at time `t` and compare:

- stress peak without confirmed rollover;
- rollover without persistent same-side taking;
- rollover plus persistent taking and declining executable price response;
- volatility-, spread-, trend-, and impulse-matched controls.

Measure continuation and reversal probabilities, executable entry price, maximum favorable/adverse excursion, spread capture or crossing cost, fill probability, fees, latency, and slippage over declared horizons. Require replenishing orders to persist or execute; displayed depth that cancels before interaction should not count as counter-liquidity.

The claim is weakened if the signal is contemporaneous with or later than the reversal, if continuation dominates after conditioning on informed flow, if alternate depth bands reverse the result, if book-data loss explains the pattern, or if any gross effect disappears at executable prices.
