# REJ-002 — Naive cross-venue perp funding capture as a standalone edge

> Source-reported rejection, not an independently reproduced EdgeLab finding.

## Rejected claim

A small systematic trader can reliably monetize the funding-rate difference between perpetual contracts on two venues by holding offsetting positions, with delta neutrality making the return sufficiently stable after costs.

## Decision and evidence

The [source thread](https://x.com/ArbStHubert/status/2040132619413979596), captured in `KI-20260913-006`, reports approximately four months of unsuccessful implementation: gains of roughly 3–6% in some months were subsequently given back, leaving the approach around break-even. The author attributes failure to noisy relative-funding forecasts, fees, execution, added basis complexity, and cross-venue balance management.

EdgeLab rejects only the naive standalone claim for reuse without a new test. The decision date is 2026-09-13. The source supplies no code, venue list for the failed variant, position history, sample dates, benchmark, forecast specification, cost decomposition, or independently auditable PnL, so it cannot establish universal non-existence.

## Scope

The reported failure concerns one practitioner's perp–perp implementation and appears to concern economic trading PnL excluding its separate usefulness for points and volume generation. The following remain distinct and unresolved:

- deterministic capture when upcoming funding is already known and sufficient after all costs;
- a genuinely predictive relative-funding model with causal venue inputs;
- spot–perp convergence and funding capture;
- dirty-carry portfolio construction;
- incentive farming where rewards, not trading PnL, are the explicit payoff.

Delta neutrality removes only first-order matched price exposure. It does not remove basis divergence, asynchronous fills, liquidation and margin risk, funding-rule changes, transfer delays, venue failure, counterparty exposure, or capital fragmentation.

## Reopening conditions

Reconsider the rejected implementation only with a prespecified, reproducible design that:

1. predicts the funding spread using information available before each venue's settlement cutoff;
2. marks both legs at executable prices and models asynchronous entry and exit;
3. separates funding, basis convergence, trading PnL, fees, financing, borrow, slippage, liquidation loss, and incentives;
4. simulates venue-specific funding formulas, caps, intervals, margin rules, transfers, and balance constraints;
5. survives chronological holdouts, regime and venue splits, parameter perturbations, and realistic capacity limits;
6. remains positive without relying on unpriced points or token rewards unless those rewards are explicitly the tested payoff.
