# MEC-001 — Passive liquidity absorption

## Proposed participant sequence

Aggressive traders repeatedly cross the spread. Passive participants supply or replenish liquidity at nearby prices. Same-side traded quantity stays elevated while executable price progress decays. Aggressors may then stop, unwind, or be overtaken by opposing flow.

## Distinguishing observations

The mechanism is more credible if quote/depth behavior shows persistence or replenishment and if response decay is not explained by volatility, spread, or event-window arithmetic alone.

### Side-specific book sequence

The observation captured in `KI-20260913-005` proposes a more granular sequence. For an aggressive-buy impulse, asks are the consumed side and bids are the opposite side; reverse the labels for selling. The consumed side may initially lose near-touch liquidity, retreat to farther prices, and accompany a widening spread. Absorption becomes more plausible only when, despite continued same-side taking, liquidity on the consumed side persistently replenishes or reconcentrates nearer the touch while executable price response weakens. A one-snapshot depth increase is insufficient because displayed liquidity may cancel before interaction.

The proposed “price-depth standard deviation” is not yet a defined signal. Quantity-weighted price distance from the touch, cross-level variability of displayed size, and time-series variability of aggregate depth are different objects and must be tested separately.

## Falsifiers

No replenishment or stable passive capacity is visible; the effect vanishes after volatility and spread controls; or apparent absorption is caused by timestamp/book reconstruction errors. Additional falsifiers are replenishing orders that cancel before being reached, continuation through the supposed counter-liquidity, or a result that disappears under alternative fixed-bps depth bands and tick-size normalization.
